# Common questions about learning paths

## "Can I skip the fundamentals and start with the framework?"

You can start, but you will stall. Framework confusion is nearly always language
confusion wearing a costume — React errors are JavaScript errors, Django errors
are Python errors. Learners who skip ahead can assemble things that work and
cannot fix them when they break, which is most of the job.

The exception: a short taste of a framework early, to see where you are heading,
is motivating and harmless. The problem is trying to build seriously on it.

## "Why does my roadmap put something boring before something exciting?"

Because the exciting thing depends on it. SkillForge orders by prerequisite
depth, not by importance or interest. Deep learning may be the highest-value
gap on your path and still sit in phase seven, because gradient descent,
regularisation and evaluation discipline all have to come first for it to mean
anything.

A roadmap that put the interesting thing first would be more fun and would not
work.

## "How long should each phase take?"

Estimates assume steady part-time study — roughly eight hours a week. They scale
with how far you are from the required level, not with the topic's reputation
for difficulty.

Treat them as rough. The variance between individuals is larger than the
variance between topics.

## "Everything is marked locked. Where do I start?"

That means the skills your goal names all sit behind prerequisites you have not
started. Look at phase one of your roadmap — those are the entry points, and
they are unblocked by definition.

If that still looks intimidating, the honest read is that your target role is
several layers away, which is useful information rather than a problem.

## "I already know some of this. Do I have to redo it?"

No. Record what you know on your profile and take the assessment for it. A
demonstrated skill drops out of the roadmap and everything depending on it moves
closer.

Self-reported levels are treated as provisional until an assessment confirms
them — not out of distrust, but because the gap between "I've used it" and "I
can use it unaided" is exactly what the roadmap needs to get right.

## "Why do I have to keep re-taking assessments?"

Because knowledge decays, and a skill you passed six months ago and never
revisited is not a reliable claim. SkillForge schedules re-assessment per skill
at increasing intervals, timed to just before you would likely have forgotten.

This is spaced repetition applied to skills rather than facts. It is the
difference between a platform that tracks what you once studied and one that
tracks what you can currently do.

## "Should I learn breadth or depth first?"

Breadth at each layer, depth over time. Skills at the same prerequisite depth
reinforce each other and can be studied in parallel. Going deep on one branch
before touching the others tends to produce narrow capability and a long wait
before you can build anything real.
