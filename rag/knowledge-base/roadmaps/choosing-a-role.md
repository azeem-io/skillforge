# How to choose a target role

Choosing a target role is not a permanent commitment. It is a decision about
what to study for the next few months. The roadmap changes when the goal
changes, and nothing already learned is lost — the four technology roles here
overlap heavily in their foundations.

## Start from what you already have, not from what sounds exciting

The cheapest role to reach is the one whose requirements you have already
partly met. Someone with Python, NumPy and basic statistics is most of the way
into the AI Engineer foundations and barely started on DevOps. Both are
reachable; one is reachable sooner.

This is why SkillForge scores readiness against every role rather than asking
students to guess. Readiness is the share of a role's required skills already
demonstrated at or above the level the role asks for. A student can be 40%
ready for one role and 8% for another purely because of where they started.

Readiness is a measure of distance, not of suitability. A low score means the
path is longer, not that the role is wrong.

## Overlap means an early choice is cheap

The four roles share a common core: programming fundamentals, version control,
and an understanding of how programs talk over a network. Time spent on those
counts toward every role. Divergence happens later — model training for AI
Engineer, browser rendering and state for Frontend, data modelling and API
design for Backend, orchestration and infrastructure for DevOps.

The practical consequence: if two roles are close in readiness, pick either.
The first phases of both roadmaps will look nearly identical, and the decision
can be revisited once the shared foundations are done.

## When readiness should not decide

Readiness is one input. Three situations override it:

- **A role you would not enjoy.** Reaching a role you dislike faster is not a
  win. DevOps and Frontend demand very different daily work — one is systems
  and failure modes, the other is users and interfaces.
- **A large gap that is one skill wide.** Sometimes a low score is caused by
  missing a single foundational skill that unlocks many others. The gap looks
  wide and is actually shallow. Check the first phase of the roadmap: if it has
  one or two entries, the distance is smaller than the percentage suggests.
- **A specific job you are aiming at.** If a real posting names the role, that
  posting decides, and the roadmap tells you the distance.

## How to read a comparison

When comparing roles, look at three numbers together:

1. **Readiness** — how much of the role is already met.
2. **Skills remaining** — the absolute size of the gap. A 20% readiness on a
   small role can be fewer skills than 40% on a large one.
3. **First phase width** — how many skills are startable right now. A wide
   first phase means progress will feel fast early.

A role that scores lower on readiness but has a narrow, immediately startable
first phase is often the better near-term choice.

## Common mistakes

Choosing the role with the highest salary in the abstract, without checking the
distance. Choosing AI Engineer because it is the most discussed, when the
foundations required — programming, data handling, statistics — are longer than
they appear. Switching goals every few weeks, which resets nothing technically
but prevents any single path from reaching the phase where the work gets
interesting.
